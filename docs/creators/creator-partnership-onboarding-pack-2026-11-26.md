No creator has been contacted and no agreement has been executed as part of preparing this document.

## 1. Operating approach

NexaFilm's first creator pilot should use a **non-exclusive, title-specific, time-limited licence**. The creator retains ownership. NexaFilm receives only the expressly listed rights for the chosen film, versions, territories, business model and term.

The agreement must not use blank fields as implied permission. A blank, ambiguous or unchecked scope item means **not granted** until the parties expressly complete it in writing.

### Pilot guardrails

- Start with one independently produced Tamil short film and one verified rights owner / authorised licensor.
- Prefer a non-exclusive arrangement. Do not ask for or imply a transfer of copyright or ownership.
- Decide exact territories, start/end dates, exploitation modes and commercial terms before execution. Do not assume worldwide rights, perpetual rights, or a default revenue split.
- Treat subscription, ad-supported, transactional/pay-per-view, free/ad-free promotional viewing and other monetisation methods as separate choices.
- Obtain separate, express permission for trailers, clips, posters, stills, thumbnails, logos, downloads/offline viewing, dubbing, subtitle creation, editing, and any promotional reuse.
- Do not use the film, script, audio, captions, artwork or metadata to train, fine-tune, evaluate or otherwise supply third-party AI/ML systems. Any future AI processing requires a separately negotiated written opt-in.
- Allow technical vendors to process the film only to the extent necessary to host, secure, encode, package and deliver the expressly licensed stream, under confidentiality/security terms and without independent exploitation.
- No public preview, provider upload, paid provider subscription, new credential, payment, Production operation or playback-flag change is authorised by this pack.
- Keep confidential contracts and identity/authority documents in approved restricted legal storage. Do not commit them to GitHub, paste them into public issues, or place them in general-purpose chat.

## 6. Legal and process reference notes

This draft is structured to force the parties to identify the work, the rights actually granted, the duration, territory and commercial consideration. The Indian Copyright Act, 1957 includes section 30 on licences by copyright owners and section 30A applying section 19 to licences with necessary adaptations. The official text should be checked in its current form by counsel before this template is used:

- [Copyright Office — Copyright Act, 1957, Chapter VI (Licences)](https://copyright.gov.in/Copyright_Act_1957/chapter_vi.html)
- [IP India — Copyright Act, 1957, Section 19 (Mode of Assignment)](https://ipindia.gov.in/acts/copyright-act-1957/section-19-2)
- [IP India — Copyright Act, 1957, Section 30 (Licences by owners of copyright)](https://ipindia.gov.in/acts/copyright-act-1957/section-30-4)

These notes are a drafting aid, not a legal opinion. The exact effect of statutory provisions, contributor rights, music rights, collecting-society obligations, tax treatment, electronic execution, consumer rules and territorial restrictions depends on the facts and must be reviewed by qualified counsel.
